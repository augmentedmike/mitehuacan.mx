# Field Playbook — Getting Fiesta Vendors Into the Directory

*2026-07-21. How the founder-led (Michael) acquisition conversation actually goes when
signing a business into the free Fiesta Vendor Directory — plus the QR/WhatsApp motions
that need no conversation at all. There is **no sales team**; this is founder-led and
QR-driven. Grounded in `business/research/07-adversarial-review-from-puebla.md`
(cultural realities), the Phase 2 PRD, and `financials/phase2-revenue-capture.md`.*

> **The key idea:** this is **not a sales call — it's a 2-minute free sign-up.** Because
> listing is free, there is no money to object to. The hard conversion (the paid boost)
> happens **later, warm, over WhatsApp**, after the vendor has already gotten free leads.
> The founder's job in the field is not to *sell* — it's to *seed supply* as fast as
> possible.

---

## 1. Three acquisition motions (only the first is a "call")

| Motion | Who | Effort | When to use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Founder walk-in** (script below) | Michael, in person | High | Seed the first ~50 vendors; densest fiesta-vendor corridors |
| **B. QR flyer / passive** | Anyone; combis; tianguis; FB | Zero conversation | Scale after the model is proven; leave-behind everywhere |
| **C. DENUE WhatsApp seed** | Michael, from the 28,727-row layer | Low, remote | Warm-ish message to fiesta-category businesses we already have |

All three end in the **same** place: the business scans/opens a link, lists itself free
in <5 min, and gets a WhatsApp edit link back.

## 2. The walk-in conversation (Motion A) — ~3 minutes

**Setting:** you walk into a taquizas place, panadería, mueblería de fiestas, or catch a
DJ/fotógrafo you found on Facebook. Respect the culture — do **not** rush to the pitch.

### Step 1 — Greeting & rapport (don't skip; this is Tehuacán, not SF)
> "Buenas tardes, ¿cómo está? Qué rico huele. ¿Usted hace las taquizas para fiestas?"

Let them talk. Ask about the business. Accept the Coke. *The Puebla review is explicit:
skipping this kills the interaction.*

### Step 2 — The hook (their customers, not your product)
> "Mire, le platico rápido. ¿Conoce MiTehuacán? Es la app de las rutas de combi que ya
> usa mucha gente aquí en Tehuacán. Ahora estamos ayudando a las familias a **organizar
> sus fiestas** — cumpleaños, XV, bautizos. Y cuando una familia arma su fiesta, la app
> les pregunta qué necesitan: taquiza, pastel, DJ, sillas, decoración… **y les mostramos
> los negocios de aquí.**"

Frame is always: *families planning fiestas will find you.* Never "quiero venderte
publicidad."

### Step 3 — The ask (tiny, free, now)
> "Lo que quiero es que **usted aparezca ahí, gratis.** Cuando una familia busque taquiza
> para su fiesta, que lo vean a usted. ¿Le parece si lo registro ahorita? Son dos
> minutos."

### Step 4 — Kill the fear before they raise it
> "No necesita nada — ni RFC, ni factura, ni cuenta de banco. Es como su página de
> Facebook, nomás para que lo encuentren. Solo necesito **el WhatsApp del negocio** para
> que las familias le escriban directo."

*This line is load-bearing. Fear of being "seen" by the SAT is the documented #1 reason
Mexican informal vendors refuse digital tools — name it and defuse it unprompted.*

### Step 5 — Do it for them, on the spot
Don't hand them homework. Fill the form yourself on your phone (or have them scan the QR
and you guide):
- Nombre del negocio · Categoría (taquiza) · WhatsApp · Zona/colonia · 1–2 fotos (take
  them right there) · precio desde (optional).
> "Listo. Ya aparece. Le acaba de llegar un link a su WhatsApp — con eso puede subir más
> fotos o cambiar lo que quiera, cuando quiera."

### Step 6 — Plant the upgrade seed (soft, no money today)
> "Y cuando empiece a recibir clientes de las fiestas, si algún día quiere **aparecer
> hasta arriba** cuando busquen su categoría, hay una opción para eso — pero eso es
> después. Por ahora está adentro, gratis. Yo le aviso por WhatsApp cómo le va."

**Done.** No price was quoted. No close was attempted. You seeded supply and opened a
WhatsApp channel you'll use later for the warm boost sale.

## 3. The objections (all easy, because it's free)

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| **"¿Cuánto cuesta?"** | "Aparecer es **gratis**. No le cobro nada por estar en el directorio." |
| **"¿Y esto qué es? / no lo conozco"** | *Show the live app.* "Mire, aquí están las rutas de combi… y aquí ya aparecen otros negocios. Es de aquí, de Tehuacán." Social proof beats explanation. |
| **"No tengo tiempo"** | "No se preocupe, **yo se lo lleno ahorita**. Nomás deme el WhatsApp del negocio, son dos minutos." |
| **"¿Me va a llegar el SAT / Hacienda?"** | "No, para nada. Esto **no es del gobierno**. Es como su Facebook — solo para que las familias lo encuentren. No pedimos RFC ni nada." |
| **"El dueño no está"** (family member) | "No hay problema. Con el WhatsApp del negocio lo registro, y el dueño puede editar todo después con el link que le llega. ¿Me pasa el WhatsApp?" |
| **"Ya tengo Facebook"** | "¡Perfecto, y sígalo usando! Esto es **además** — aquí lo ven las familias **en el momento en que están armando la fiesta** y necesitan taquiza. Ahí es cuando decide." |
| **"¿Cuánta gente lo usa?"** | Be honest + concrete: "Está creciendo con las rutas de combi; por eso quiero tener listos a los buenos negocios como el suyo desde ahora." Don't inflate — the Puebla review punishes fabricated numbers. |

## 4. Who to sign first (seed order)

Sign the categories a host **always** needs, so the first Fiestas have non-empty results:
**taquiza/catering → pastel/repostería → DJ/música → decoración/globos → mobiliario y
sillas → salón/jardín**, then foto/video, flores, meseros, brincolines, piñatas.

Target the **densest corridors first** (Puebla review: one person can't cover 80 routes
— go corridor by corridor, e.g. Centro, the main fiesta-supply streets).

## 5. How "listed" becomes "recommended to fiestas"

The vendor asks *how* they get recommended — here's the honest answer to give them:

- **Free tier — you show up.** Every listed vendor appears in the fiesta results for
  their **category + zona**, matched to what the host needs. Being listed *is* being
  recommendable.
- **Boost tier (later, paid) — you show up *first*.** The one-shot Destacado puts you at
  the top of your category with a "Recomendado" mark for 30 days (~$199 MXN, ~1–3% of one
  taquiza event). *This is the only paid thing, and it's optional, and it comes after
  they've seen free leads.*

So the founder never sells "recommendation" — recommendation is free. The founder sells
**priority**, later, to a vendor who's already warm.

---

## 6. Why this beats a real sales call (the strategic point)

The Puebla review says a cold close in this market takes **3–5 visits**, the owner is
often absent, and micro-businesses have no subscription culture. A traditional sponsorship
sales call runs straight into all of that. This motion **dodges every one of them**:

- **No money → no objection → no multi-visit close.** Sign-up is one visit.
- **Owner absent? Fine** — register with the business WhatsApp; owner edits later.
- **No subscription** — nothing recurring is being proposed.
- **The real sale is deferred and warm** — by the time you pitch the boost over WhatsApp,
  the vendor has *received free fiesta leads* and is converting on evidence, not on trust.

That is the entire reason the directory is free: it turns the hard cold sale into an easy
free sign-up now, and an easy warm upsell later.

## 7. The later warm boost pitch (over WhatsApp, weeks later)

Not a field call — a WhatsApp message once they've gotten leads:
> "Qué tal Don [X]. Vi que le han llegado [N] familias buscando taquiza este mes por
> MiTehuacán 👍 Si quiere aparecer **hasta arriba** cuando busquen taquiza en su zona, le
> puedo activar el Destacado por 30 días. ¿Le mando el link para activarlo?"

They tap → OXXO/card pay-link → boost auto-publishes. No visit, no negotiation — because
you're not selling a promise anymore, you're selling *more of something already working.*
