## Reconciliation from adversarial review [→ full review](challenges/phase-b--business-directory--premium-profiles.md)

| Assumption | Projection | Adversarial challenge | Adjusted range |
|---|---|---|---|
| $300/mo self-serve adoption | 238 profiles by month 24 | Micro-businesses won't self-serve a paid tool. Every signup requires hand-holding. [1] | Requires dedicated sales |
| Cross-sell rate from Phase A | 60% | If Phase A close rate is 5-10%, the base to cross-sell is much smaller. | 20-30% |
| Value prop vs. free Facebook page | "Transit integration" is the differentiator | Businesses not on a combi route get only a listing on an unknown platform. Facebook is free and already has their audience. [2] | Thin for non-route businesses |
| 15% annual churn | Profile is "permanent" | Digital products for micro-businesses in Mexico don't see 15% churn unless they're indispensable tools (banking, WhatsApp). | 30-40% |

**Net effect:** 80-120 profiles by month 24 (not 238). Year 2 revenue $250K-300K (not $651K). Works only if bundled with Phase A sales visit — standalone self-serve will not grow.

[1] Adversarial difference #2: "Subscription" is a foreign concept. Self-serve assumes digital payment behavior that does not exist for this demographic.
[2] Adversarial difference #4: Facebook is the internet for most of Tehuacán. A standalone profile on a new platform competes with a free, well-understood, already-used Facebook page.
