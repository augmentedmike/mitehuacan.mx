# Adversarial Review

## Phase G — Employment & Community

#### Assertion: 48 accounts by month 24 at ~$450/mo average

**Challenge:** This phase is the furthest out (month 19) and the most
speculative. The job board market in Tehuacán is dominated by Facebook
groups (there are multiple "empleos en Tehuacán" Facebook groups with
50,000+ members, all free), word of mouth, and the occasional OCC or indeed
listing for professional roles. A paid job board for Tehuacán-specific roles
needs to offer something these free channels do not. For most employers in
Tehuacán, Facebook groups work fine and cost nothing.

**Challenge on community events:** Events are promoted via Facebook events
and WhatsApp forwards. These are free, ubiquitous, and well-understood.
Paying $200/event for promotion on a new platform is a tough ask.

**Adjusted estimate:** 20-30 accounts by month 24. Average $300/mo (per-post
cheaper than subscriptions). Year 2 revenue: $25,000-35,000 (not $57,000).

## But Here Is What the Projection Gets Right

The adversarial review is not a prediction. It is a stress test. Let me be
fair about what is actually strong in these projections:

1. **The unit economics work if volume materializes.** $500/mo from a business
   that gets 10 additional customers per month is genuinely good ROI.
   The fundamental value prop (discovery during transit) is real.

2. **The sales process is well-designed for digital close.** WhatsApp contract
   + click-to-sign + instant pin publication is genuinely innovative for this
   market. It solves the "when can I see results?" objection elegantly.

3. **Phase 0 (QR stickers in combis) is the right starting point.** Organic
   discovery via QR codes on combis creates a usage loop that requires zero
   marketing spend. This is a real advantage over competitors who need to
   buy ads.

4. **The DENUE data is a real moat.** Having 28,727 pre-located businesses
   is a legitimate operational advantage. The prospect lists exist and are
   actionable. Most local advertising startups in Mexico skip this step.

5. **Barter sponsorships (Phase 0) are culturally smart.** Trading visibility
   for wall space is a concept Mexican micro-businesses understand and will
   accept. It seeds the map at zero cash cost.

## What the Projection Needs to Fix

1. **Double the sales cycle, halve the close rate.** Adjust months 1-6 for
   realistic ramp. Recalculate Year 1 with 5-10% cold close rate.

2. **Add a collections cost assumption.** If 40% of payments arrive late or
   require follow-up, that costs time and money. Budget for it.

3. **Foundation pricing must match business/marketing-plan.md.** Use $325/mo average, not
   $500/mo, for the first 100 sponsors. Recalculate.

4. **Add a Facebook distribution line item.** Either a Facebook integration
   budget or a content marketing cost. Without it, adoption is capped.

5. **Model seasonality.** Add January and September dips. Real revenue is
   lumpy. Investors prefer a projection that acknowledges this.

6. **Lower the churn assumption.** Use 50-60% for Year 1 renewals. The plan
   can beat this, but it should plan for it.

7. **Chunk the sales territory.** One salesperson cannot cover all of
   Tehuacán's 80 routes effectively in 6 hours/day. Propose a route-by-route
   rollout plan starting with the 10 highest-density corridors.

8. **Add cash collection infrastructure.** Plan for a weekly cobranza route.
   If you accept cash payments, someone needs to physically collect them.
